The Pomodoro timer runs a focus block, then a break, and repeats. The point is to make
starting easy: you are not committing to finishing something, only to working on it until
the timer stops.

## Block types and durations

The timer runs three kinds of block, picked with the segmented control under the dial.

| Block | Default | You can set |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focus | 25 minutes | 1 to 180 minutes |
| Short break | 5 minutes | 1 to 60 minutes |
| Long break | 15 minutes | 1 to 120 minutes |

A long break replaces a short one every fourth focus block by default, and that interval
can be set anywhere from 1 to 12.

Your durations sync across devices, so the timer behaves the same on your phone and in a
browser. The defaults are the classic Pomodoro numbers and are a reasonable place to
start, but 25 minutes is a convention rather than a finding. If you routinely stop early,
shorten the block rather than fighting it.

## Pinning a task

You can pin a task to a session, which is worth doing. It records what the session was
for, and it is what makes [Focus statistics](/marvelist/premium/) meaningful rather than
just a count of sessions.

## The timer keeps time properly

The countdown is wall-clock based, so it survives a page reload and a browser throttling a
background tab. Coming back to the tab after ten minutes shows ten minutes gone, not a
timer that paused when you looked away.

## Notifications

You are notified at the end of every block, focus and break alike, so you can leave the
tab or put the phone down.

On iOS there is also a Live Activity, which puts the running timer on your Lock Screen and
in the Dynamic Island. See [Widgets](/marvelist/apple-devices/widgets/).

## Statistics

[Premium](/marvelist/premium/) adds focus statistics and insights: what you have actually
spent time on, rather than what you planned to.
