## Custom questions

You can ask guests extra questions as part of the RSVP: dietary requirements, song
requests, which coach they are taking, anything you would otherwise be chasing by email.
Add them from **Manage Event → Questions**.

Questions appear on the RSVP page, so the answer arrives with the reply rather than in a
separate conversation.

## Question types

| Type | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| Short text | A name, a song title, a seat preference |
| Paragraph | Anything that needs a sentence or two |
| Single choice | One option from a list you set |
| Multiple choice | Any number of options from a list you set |
| Yes / No | A straight answer, easy to count |
| Number | Nights staying, children coming, anything you will add up |

Choice questions take one option per line when you create them. Use them wherever the
answers are knowable in advance: a Single choice between three menu options gives you
something you can total, where the same question as Short text gives you forty spellings
of "chicken".

## Required, and only if attending

Each question has two switches.

**Required** stops the RSVP being submitted without an answer. Use it sparingly. A required
question that someone cannot answer is a person who abandons the form and never comes back
to it.

**Only if attending** hides the question from anyone who declines. Worth turning on for
most questions: there is no point asking someone what they want for dinner immediately
after they have told you they are not coming.

## Reading the answers

Answers arrive attached to each guest, alongside their RSVP and their dietary
requirements, so a single view tells you who is coming and what they said.

This is where the value shows up. One screen with every dietary requirement on it is the
difference between a caterer conversation that takes five minutes and one that takes an
evening of scrolling through messages. Guest exports can include the answers, so the
caterer can have the list rather than your summary of it.

## Writing good questions

Two things worth doing:

- Ask only what you will act on. Every question is friction on the RSVP page, and friction
  lowers your response rate.
- Be specific. "Any dietary requirements?" gets "no" from people who then mention a
  serious allergy on the night. "Any allergies or dietary requirements we should know
  about?" does better.

## Changing questions later

Editing questions after people have replied leaves you with answers to the old question
and gaps for anyone who already responded. Get the questions right before you send
invitations where you can.
